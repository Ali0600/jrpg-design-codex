## Mechanics candidates

### A collection book that pays for every row, every page, and a last page you can only trade for
cat: Exploration & Rewards
how: Every area has a page in the Collectopaedia with a row for each kind of collectable found there, and completing a row pays a gem or a piece of gear, as does filling the whole page. The rewards climb with the region, from rank II gems in the first colony to rank V on the last island, and each page's prize is named gear. The final page, Other, cannot be filled by exploring at all: its seven items are only traded for, by named townspeople once the party's standing in their region is high enough.
loop: pick up collectables while crossing an area → a row fills and pays a gem → the page fills and pays gear → the last page sends you back to town to earn the trades
notes: The shape worth copying is the ROW: a small, short-horizon goal inside a long collection, so a player who will never finish a page still gets paid for finishing three flowers. The rewards scale with the region, so the book stays worth attention all game instead of paying trinkets. And the last page is a clever handoff: when exploring runs out, the collection becomes a reason to raise your standing with a town and learn its trade economy. It pairs with M221, whose colony consumes the same collectables as donations, and XC3 later reworked the book into card requests sent to its colonies (M055).

### A gem furnace where the skill is stopping at 99%
cat: Progression & Upgrades
how: Gems are crafted by combining two to eight ether crystals or cylinders; any quality that crosses 100% becomes a gem, and whatever stays under 100% can be kept as a single-quality cylinder for another session. Two party members work the furnace, and their affinity sets how many turns they get, from 3–5 at the lowest stage to 10–15 at the highest, while the rest of the party can step in with support. A quality pushed past 200% gives a gem one grade higher, and past 300% (Mega Heat) gives two. So the best gems come from failing on purpose first: refine a quality into cylinders as close to 99% as possible, then combine two of them with a high-affinity pair.
loop: gather crystals from deposits and monsters → refine the quality you want into cylinders just under 100% → combine two with your closest-bonded pair → cross 300% for two top-grade gems → bonds between party members become crafting power
notes: Two ideas for the owner's pillar. The refinement is an upgrade track inside the crafting system itself: a player who learns to stop at 99% makes better gems than one who crafts greedily, which is knowledge as a reward. And party relationships feed a non-combat system, since the turns come from the pair's affinity and the support from everyone else's, so time spent bonding characters pays off at the furnace. Copy the rule that a near miss is kept rather than wasted; drop the save-and-reset habit the guide recommends by making the top end less random.
pointers: [gf:65405 §The Creation Process, zerokid v1.33] [gf:65405 §Crafting Mega Heat Gems, zerokid v1.33] [wiki:xenoblade.fandom.com/Gem Crafting]
row: M287
- A gem takes two to eight crafting items; shared qualities add together, and once any quality passes 100% no more items can be added [gf:65405 §Combining Items, zerokid v1.33]
- Crystals carry up to four qualities and cylinders one, each quality at most 99% [gf:65405 §Combining Items, zerokid v1.33]
- The engineer decides the flames: a strong flame raises one quality 3–10%, a medium flame every quality 1–5%, a gentle flame the cylinder gauge 30–70%, and each filled gauge is one more cylinder to keep [gf:65405 §The Creation Process, zerokid v1.33]
- A Fever multiplies a turn's gains 3 to 10 times, and support from the rest of the party, based on their affinity, is often what makes the strongest gems [gf:65405 §The Creation Process, zerokid v1.33]
- The wiki's turn formula, between (affinity + 250) / 750 + 3 and affinity / 500 + 5 for a pair's affinity of 0 to 5,000, gives the guide's 3–5 turns at the lowest stage and 10–15 at the highest [wiki:xenoblade.fandom.com/Gem Crafting] [gf:65405 §The Creation Process, zerokid v1.33]
- Past 200% a gem comes out a grade higher and past 300% two full-strength gems come out; the wiki notes that falling just short at 99% is what makes those easier [wiki:xenoblade.fandom.com/Gem Crafting]
- The guide's Mega Heat method: refine the quality into cylinders with a gentle-flame pair (Sharla shooting, Riki engineering) to just above 80%, then combine two with a strong-flame, high-affinity pair such as Shulk and Reyn, since two 99% cylinders are the best possible start at 198% [gf:65405 §Crafting Mega Heat Gems, zerokid v1.33]
- Crafting becomes portable through a quest: the Gem Man in Colony 9 builds a Mobile Furnace once the party brings an Ultra Small Reactor, earned by raising every one of Colony 6's development tracks to its first level [wiki:xenoblade.fandom.com/Gem Crafting]

### Trade stock that opens with a region's affinity, and a bonus for overpaying
cat: Economy & Currency
how: Named townspeople trade instead of selling: you pick an item from their stock and hand over something of equal or greater value, and each item's trade value is tied to its sale price, raised 25% by two party skills. What they will offer depends on the party's affinity with their region, one of five sectors rated out of five stars and raised by finishing that sector's quests. Hand over enough to cover both the item and the townsperson's fixed bonus item, and they add the bonus: an overtrade, possible at any affinity.
loop: finish a region's quests → its affinity rises and its people's trade stock widens → trade a found item worth more than you need → take home the overtrade bonus too
notes: The owner's "shops have upgrades too" pillar applied to people instead of shops: a region's trade stock is an upgrade the player earns by helping its residents, and the rarest collectables sit behind it. The overtrade rule is the detail to steal, since a published bonus for overpaying turns a pile of loot into a targeted offer, and the arithmetic (item plus bonus value) is simple enough to plan. Keep the value rule visible: here a trade's worth is a multiple of the sale price, and the player needs that number to plan an overtrade. XC3 later scaled the same idea up to whole colonies whose affinity unlocks quests, shops and services (M056).
verbs: Traded
pointers: [gf:64875 §Basics, zerokid v1.12] [wiki:xenoblade.fandom.com/Trading]
row: M288
- The world has five affinity sectors with their own colours: Colony 9; Colony 6 with Bionis' Leg; Central Bionis (Satorl Marsh, Makna Forest, Frontier Village); Upper Bionis (Eryth Sea, Alcamoth, Valak Mountain); and the Hidden Village sector (Fallen Arm, Mechonis Field) [gf:64875 §Basics, zerokid v1.12]
- A townsperson counts toward the sector where they were first met, unless invited to Colony 6, which moves them into its sector [gf:64875 §Basics, zerokid v1.12]
- Two skills raise what the party's items are worth in a trade, Shulk's Hero's Privilege by 20% and Riki's Friends Now! by 5%, 25% together [gf:64875 §Basics, zerokid v1.12] [wiki:xenoblade.fandom.com/Trading]
- The guide's overtrade example: for an item worth 4,000 G and a bonus worth 8,000 G the offer must be worth at least 12,000 G, and anything from 4,000 to 11,999 G gets only the item [gf:64875 §Basics, zerokid v1.12]
- The wiki sets trade values at the sale price for materials and ten times the sale price for weapons, armour and collectables, and notes that a townsperson's stock can change when they move [wiki:xenoblade.fandom.com/Trading]
