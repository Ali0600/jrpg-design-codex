## Mechanics candidates

### Merit awards: side quests pay stamps that buy upgrades to how you play, a tier at a time
cat: Progression & Upgrades
how: Every errand and bounty hunt pays its listed reward plus merit stamps, and a full stamp card becomes a merit card to spend at Swift Solutions on a passive ability. The awards come in three tiers, and every award in a tier must be bought before the next tier opens. The cheap first tier is mostly about getting around (a jump, faster movement on the world map and at sea, more from foraging, sneaking up on enemies); the dearer tiers add a speed boost for the dragon and health restored by walking alongside more experience, better drops and cheaper shops, and end on an eight-card award that adds 100 HP and 100 MP to Oliver.
loop: take errands and bounties → every reward comes with stamps → full cards buy out a tier of awards and open the next → feels good because the side quests you did become permanent upgrades to how you explore, fight and shop
notes: A second currency that side quests pay on top of their normal reward, and that buys only upgrades to the act of playing, is the owner's pillar in a single system. Two details are worth copying. The movement awards sit in the cheapest tier and a tier must be bought out before the next, so the first things the quest board buys are better ways to explore, and the experience boosts cannot be rushed past them. And every award is permanent and passive, so no quest's payout goes stale the way a consumable does. It sits beside KH2's Drive Forms (M250), which pay traversal abilities for using each form, and Ys VIII's cartography rewards (M234), which pay exploring with more exploring; here the payer is the quest board.

## Minigame candidates

### The Crypt Casino: prize ranks that open with the story
p: After the party finishes the Tombstone Trail chapter a casino opens there, selling chips for guilders. It runs a slot machine, blackjack, Double Cross and Platoon, a card game in which a hand is split into piles that battle the dealer's. A set of Platoon rounds costs 200 chips, and it is the game both the wiki and the Platoon guide use to win chips in quantity.
r: Sixteen prizes in four ranks: C and B from the first visit (Crownberries, Big Babana, Sprite Dew and a Medal of Agility; the Bluster Blade, Great Sage's Secret and two familiar tickets, the Tin-Man at 99,999 chips), A after the Manna event (the four Jumbo drops, 3,000 chips each) and S after the credits (four tickets that replay the story's scenes, 75,000 chips for the set).
l: A prize shelf staged by the story gives a venue three reasons to come back: the useful items are there on the first visit, the Jumbo drops arrive late, and the post-credits prizes are replays of the story rather than power, because power no longer matters by then. The dearest prize of all is a familiar, so the longest chip grind pays into the game's main collection instead of a trinket. Copy the staging and put a collectible at the top; the risk is a 99,999-chip price that only a player who has learned Platoon will ever reach.

### The Solosseum: ranks of back-to-back fights, each paying an item and gold
p: An arena in the Temple of Trials where the party fights battles back to back for prizes, three of them at the first rank. Each rank opens once the one below it is won, and the last, Rank S, also waits for the credits.
r: Every rank pays an item and gold, from a Dragon Tusk and 2,000 G at Rank E to the Mandragorer Ticket, for a legendary familiar, and 30,000 G at Rank S.
l: A ladder of fights where every rung pays and the top rung is a familiar rather than a stronger sword gives an arena a reason to be revisited all game, because each rank can be tried whenever the party feels ready. For the owner's game, show each rank's prize before the fight so the climb has a target, and put a collectible at the top instead of a stat item.

### Upgrades
- Merit awards, tier one, in cards: Jumping Jack 1 (a jump), Jack-Be-Nimble 1 (faster on the world map), Little Jack Horner 1 (more from foraging), Jack-in-the-Box 2 (easier to sneak up on enemies), Jackpot 2 (more glims in battle), Jolly Jack-Tar 1 (a faster ship). The effects agree across the sources; two of the prices do not (see below) [wiki:ninokuni.fandom.com/Merit Awards] [gf:65877 §Merit Awards (ITM-3), A_Backdated_Future v1.07] [gf:65886 §Errands and Bounty Hunts, Berserker_Blade v0.50]
- Tier two: Cheapjack 3 (cheaper shops), Crackerjack 3 (more experience), Jack-o'-Lantern 3 (more drops), Jackpot II 3 (glims left on the field collected after a win), Jack-at-a-Pinch 3 (a cheaper continue after a defeat), Flapjack 2 (a speed boost while flying the dragon Tengri) [wiki:ninokuni.fandom.com/Merit Awards] [gf:65877 §Merit Awards (ITM-3), A_Backdated_Future v1.07]
- Tier three: Jack of Hearts 4 (easier to befriend familiars), Jack-Amend-All 4 (health restored by walking), Crackerjack II 4 (more experience again), Jack-of-all-Trades 5 (lower MP costs), Jack-o'-Lantern II 5 (rarer drops), Jack the Giant Killer 8 [wiki:ninokuni.fandom.com/Merit Awards] [gf:65877 §Merit Awards (ITM-3), A_Backdated_Future v1.07]; noz3r0's table gives the same 5 and 8 for the last two [gf:65876 §Errands (0400), noz3r0 v0.92]
- By the time the party can fly, one walkthrough expects enough merit cards for every award in the second tier [gf:65904 §Boss Battle (before The Vault of Tears), Malduke v1.2.0]
- Treats are sweets fed to familiars to raise stats such as attack and magic defence [wiki:ninokuni.fandom.com/Treats]; one guide recommends the casino's Crownberries and Big Babana because they make the most powerful treats [gf:65877 §Walkthrough (the first casino note), A_Backdated_Future v1.07]
- The first use of a Familiar Retreat gives three Great Sage's Secrets and items for metamorphosis [gf:65904 §Boss Battle (the Temple of Trials test), Malduke v1.2.0], which one guide lists as three of each Jumbo drop, the items that take a familiar to its third form [gf:65877 §The first Familiar Retreat, A_Backdated_Future v1.07]
- A familiar's second form is always the same, but its third and final form is a choice between two, which differ mainly in element, growth pattern and the kinds of tricks they learn [gf:65877 §lines 933–956, A_Backdated_Future v1.07]
- A first metamorphosis usually comes between levels 10 and 20 and the second 10 to 15 levels later; the first needs a drop matching the familiar's sign (a star drop for a star familiar) and the second a stronger drop of that sign. Each resets the familiar to level 1, with stats only some 10 to 20 points higher by the walkthrough's estimate, so the choice is between a familiar strong early and one strong late [gf:65877 §lines 933–956, A_Backdated_Future v1.07]
- Treats raise a familiar's six stats by at most 50 points combined, 10 for each of its five familiarity hearts filled, and the hearts are filled by treats too; every battle empties a stomach slot of every familiar, whether it fought or not [gf:65877 §lines 746–755, A_Backdated_Future v1.07]
- A familiar learns at most eight tricks and must forget one to learn another, and only a few can be set for battle [gf:65877 §lines 398–405, A_Backdated_Future v1.07]
- The Familiar Retreat stores up to 400 familiars [gf:65877 §lines 2740–2759, A_Backdated_Future v1.07]
