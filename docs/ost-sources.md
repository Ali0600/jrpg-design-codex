# JRPG soundtracks: the best official sources

<!-- Measured 2026-09-09. This is not a research digest: scripts/digest_lint.mjs scans docs/research/ only, and this file sits outside its pointer grammar by design. -->

The owner wanted the original soundtracks of the codex's highest-ranked games in the best
quality available, as reference audio for Google Flow Music. This file answers that, one row per
game.

**How the 37 games were chosen.** The union of the top 20 by each of the codex's three rankings —
Metascore, Metacritic user score and GameFAQs user rating — measured from the page's own
`BASE_GAMES` data on 2026-09-09. Seven games sit in all three lists, nine in two, twenty-one in
one; the table keeps that order so the consensus games come first.

**Sourcing.** Official or licensed sources only: publisher stores, Bandcamp, Steam and GOG
soundtrack bundles, lossless download stores, the publishers' own YouTube channels, streaming
services, the Nintendo Music app, and CDs. Every row gives two routes — the best official *file*
and the quickest official *free listen* — so a game whose only lossless copy is an out-of-print
import CD still has a usable source. "No standalone release" is a valid entry; nothing is padded.

**Rights.** Every soundtrack here is a copyrighted work. Google's guidance for Flow Music is that
uploaded audio is a rights-bearing input you must own or be licensed to use; how a bought copy is
used as a reference is the owner's call under Google's terms, not this document's.

## Publisher notes (established once, reused by every row)

- **Bandcamp** sells every purchase in MP3 V0, MP3 320, FLAC, AAC, Ogg Vorbis, ALAC, WAV and AIFF
  (Bandcamp help: <https://get.bandcamp.help/en/articles/15263234-in-which-formats-can-i-download-my-purchases>).
  Album pages state the master's bit depth and rate. Two roster games are on it (rows 16 and 36).
- **iTunes Store** purchases are "iTunes Plus": DRM-free **256 kbps AAC**
  (<https://support.apple.com/guide/itunes/aside/glosf8b7af70/12.12/windows/10>). Apple Music album
  pages double as the iTunes Store listing. For Square Enix Music, Atlus and Sega titles this is the
  best official *download*; their lossless copies are CDs.
- **OTOTOY** (<https://ototoy.jp>, Japan) is the one download store found that sells these
  soundtracks **lossless**. Confirmed album by album in the rows below on 2026-09-09: Square Enix
  Music (every roster title except Vagrant Story), Atlus, Sega, FromSoftware / Bandai Namco, Sony
  Music (Mother 2), Sony Interactive (Wild Arms), the Kingdom Hearts albums and the Xenoblade
  albums, all at 16-bit/44.1 kHz WAV / FLAC / ALAC, several at 24-bit (Xenogears *Revival Disc*,
  Xenoblade Chronicles 3, Yakuza 0, Metaphor). Not carried: Vagrant Story, Suikoden II, and
  Nintendo's in-house scores. Its how-to-buy page (<https://ototoy.jp/feature/howtobuy>) lists the
  formats as "ALAC, FLAC, WAV, MP3, AAC — choice might be limited on certain items"; with an
  account you can pay by credit card, PayPal or Amazon Pay, and without one "the payment means are
  limited to PayPal, BitCash, Moritapo and Amazon Pay" (and the files cannot be re-downloaded).
  The page says nothing about customers outside Japan, so buy one cheap album first to prove your
  card or PayPal account is accepted. The site answers automated reads with a human-verification
  page; every OTOTOY link here was read in a real browser.
- **Square Enix Music elsewhere** — Qobuz US returned only tribute albums for two searches (FF7
  Rebirth, FFX), mora.jp returned no result for the FF7 Rebirth soundtrack, and 7digital answers
  with a human-verification challenge. iTunes (AAC 256) is the best download outside Japan; the
  CDs, reissued by Square Enix and sold by the Square Enix Store, are the other lossless copy.
  Stock was checked only where a row says so.
- **Nintendo Music** (the Switch Online app; streaming only, no files; Thumbsticks' catalogue guide,
  updated 8 Sep 2026: <https://www.thumbsticks.com/every-soundtrack-nintendo-music-app/>) carries
  Tears of the Kingdom, Fire Emblem: Three Houses, Golden Sun and Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door
  (GameCube and Switch). It does **not** carry Xenoblade Chronicles 1 or 3, EarthBound, Mother 3,
  Super Mario RPG or Terranigma.
- **GOG** bundles soundtracks as "goodies" with some games, in FLAC and MP3 (The Witcher 3, row 6).
- **Streaming** (Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music) is lossy and cannot be saved as a file; it is
  the *free listen* column, never the *file* column.

## Prep for Flow Music

- **Formats.** Google documents no accepted format, size or length for Flow Music's *Add →
  Audio*; WAV and MP3 are the two formats every source agrees on, and Flow itself exports MP3,
  WAV and M4A. Upload **one short WAV first** and confirm it plays before converting a library.
- **FLAC to WAV**, keeping the source's rate and depth (`ffmpeg` 8.1 is installed at
  `/opt/homebrew/bin/ffmpeg`):

  ```bash
  ffmpeg -i in.flac -c:a pcm_s16le out.wav        # 16-bit albums
  ffmpeg -i in.flac -c:a pcm_s24le out.wav        # the 24-bit albums: Xenogears Revival Disc, Xenoblade 3, Yakuza 0, Metaphor
  for f in *.flac; do ffmpeg -i "$f" -c:a pcm_s16le "${f%.flac}.wav"; done
  ```

- **Never upsample a lossy file.** An iTunes AAC or a 320 kbps MP3 turned into WAV gains bytes,
  not quality. Try the `.m4a` as it is (Flow exports M4A, so it likely reads it); only if Flow
  refuses it, transcode once: `ffmpeg -i in.m4a -c:a libmp3lame -b:a 320k out.mp3`.
- **Cut an excerpt for a reference** — a minute is enough to describe a piece:
  `ffmpeg -ss 00:00:30 -t 60 -i in.wav -c copy clip.wav`.
- **Streaming-only scores** (Golden Sun, Paper Mario, Tears of the Kingdom in the app, Fire
  Emblem, Nintendo Music generally): there is no file. Flow's *Add → Recording* takes the
  microphone; Nintendo Music's offline downloads stay inside the app.

  of them.
