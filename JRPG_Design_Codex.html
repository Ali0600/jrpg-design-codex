Reading the codex

Every mechanic is one card. The colored orb is its category — hover the legend below. Click a card to open it. The green Reward Loop panel is the heart of each entry: what the player does, what they get, and why it feels good. That's the part to study for your exploration-reward design.

The research pipeline

The Games tab is a research queue, not just a list. Games sort by status — Researching first, then To Research, then the finished shelf — and each card shows how many mechanics and minigames it has contributed so far, so coverage gaps are visible at a glance.

  1. Queue it. Add the game with status To Research, and write the "why" as a research brief: the specific question this game should answer (the queued cards marked BRIEF show the shape).
  2. Research it. Set it to Researching, then play it or work through deep dives and wikis with the codex open. Log mechanics with + Add mechanic as you notice them.
  3. Always fill the Reward Loop. If you can't describe the loop, you haven't understood the mechanic yet.
  4. Rewards must be concrete. "Prizes and gil" is not research — name the actual items and the thresholds that earn them. Minigame cards carry a reward table for exactly this.
  5. Rate it 1–5 and decide Yes / Maybe / No. For Yes and Maybe, write how it adapts to your game, stripped of the source game's specifics.
  6. When done, set the game to Researched. Periodically filter to Yes — that cluster is the seed of your game design document.

Turning research into a design

The My Game tab is where the codex stops being a library. Everything marked Yes collects there as a shortlist, and each one gets placed into a system of the game you're building — discovery, combat, progression, venues, world. Each system has its own notes box for the rules those mechanics have to serve.

The value is in the placing. A mechanic you can't assign to a system is a mechanic you liked without knowing what it's for, and a system holding twelve entries is one you're overbuilding. Use Parked honestly — "worth loving, not worth building here" is a real answer, and a shortlist with nothing parked usually means nothing has been decided.

Your edits are saved

Ratings, decisions, notes, and new entries save automatically in this browser (when opened as a local file). They live in this browser only — use Export backup now and then to download a JSON snapshot, and Import backup to restore it or move to another computer. If your browser blocks local storage, the page still works for reading and filtering; just export before closing to keep changes.

Storage is per address. Opening the file from your disk and opening the hosted copy at ali0600.github.io/jrpg-design-codex are two separate stores — work done in one will not appear in the other. Export from the one you've been using and import into the other to bring your decisions across.
